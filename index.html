Our story

Numen dwells in all things

Numen is the Latin word for the divinity the Romans felt inside each concrete thing, the presence of this spring, this stone, this threshold. Two overtones ride along on purpose. Noumenon, Kant's thing in itself. Númenor, the island of the far-sighted.

The chain keeps that promise literally. It turns the shape of a thing into a stable fingerprint and gives each concrete object a presence on-chain. In Fan Zhen's words, the form is the substance.

PoScan · Proof of Scan

The scan is the work

Mining on Numen means scanning. Each nonce grows a deterministic asteroid, and scanning its spectral signature is the proof-of-work. The winning asteroid is rebuilt on-chain with its block. One block, one asteroid.

Scanning real-world objects is the next step. The same library that scans those asteroids reads a physical object's geometry into a stable fingerprint. Same object, any pose, any scanner, same ID, and you check it offline with no backend to trust. An NFT signs a file, and a file is one bit flip away from a new "original". We hash the thing itself. One object, one ID.

The 1800s

The stones stayed put

On Yap, money was limestone. Discs up to 3.6 m across, cut in Palau and rafted 400 km home. The big ones were too heavy to hand over, so nobody did. A payment changed the owner and nothing else. The village kept the books in its head.

One family's wealth lay on the seabed. The stone went down in a storm. The crew vouched for it, and it stayed good for generations. Nobody alive had seen it. That was a ledger of things, and it worked. But a ledger run on witnesses ends where the witnesses do.

The 2030s

Every camera becomes a scanner

A depth sensor rides in every robot, every car and every pair of AR glasses. Scanning stops being a task and turns into something that happens for free, everywhere, all day.

The sensor is not the missing piece. What is missing is the answer. A neutral function that runs offline with no backend behind it, telling you which thing this is and who holds it, plus a ledger to write it in. Point a pair of glasses at an object and its on-chain presence surfaces. That is what numen meant in the first place.

The long term

BRIDGES

Light-client bridge to Ethereum

An Ethereum contract that checks Numen finality itself. No relayer to trust, no multisig to bribe. Identity proved here, traded where the money already is.

REGISTRY

Real-world registry on mainnet

One object, one ID. The scanner that secures blocks also registers physical things, so anyone can check a shape anywhere without a tag and without a database to trust.

MACHINES

Robots, cars, AR glasses as main users

Most lookups will come from a camera, not a person. A robot checks the part it just picked, a car checks a replacement before it goes in, glasses check whatever is in view. Read latency becomes the product.

HARDWARE

Attested scans

A scan is only as honest as the scanner. Signed sensor output closes the gap between a real object and a model built to match one. Without it a match proves the shape, not the thing.

ENTROPY

Identity at 128+ bits

Collision resistance is the whole product. At 128 bits a shape is unique among everything ever manufactured, and forging a match costs more than any object is worth.

CUSTODY

The object is the key

Rebuild the private key from the object and holding becomes signing. No card, no seed phrase, no registry to trust. Lose the object and you lose the key, which is how physical property already works.

STANDARDS

did:numen in the W3C registry

A DID method anyone can resolve without permission. No custom integration for credentials, supply chain records or other chains.

MARKETS

Collectibles in the registry

Provenance that survives the object changing hands. The registry answers whether this is the piece in the record, not whether the paperwork looks right.

SPACE

Asteroids in the registry

An orbit already tells one asteroid from another, and shape models for thousands of them are published. A scan turns a shape into an ID. Ownership out there needs a ledger that belongs to no country.
